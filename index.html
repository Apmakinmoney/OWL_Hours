Macro cross-checks

Sector moves show where money is going. These signals help explain why. Mark each as falling, flat or rising over the period you’re tracking, add the level if you like, and open Learn to understand the idea behind it.

AI spending tracker

The biggest spenders are planning hundreds of billions of dollars of capital spending. Track what each says it will spend, what it spent last quarter, how fast its cloud business is growing, what it did to cash flow, and how the stock reacted.

Technical levels

The fundamentals say why the market might move. These levels show where traders are likely to react. For each major index, enter a recent close, two support levels, two resistance levels and the 50-day and 200-day averages. Levels are zones where price has reacted before, not predictions.

On the calendar

List what your readers are waiting on: Fed meetings, data releases and earnings. For each one, write what the market expects and what would count as a surprise. Events are saved with your snapshot. The weekly letter takes its events from editorial.json.

Quote of the day

One short, attributed line, tied to something in the issue. Check the exact wording and source before you publish.

Question of the day

Pick one concept question, from a reader or from your own head, and answer it in a short paragraph.

Fun fact of the issue

One memorable line gives readers a reason to open the next issue. Write your own or pick one from the shelf, and check any fact before you publish.

Newsletter notes

A draft built from your sector calls, your macro marks and what changed since your last snapshot. Edit it freely: the questions at the end are the thinking you still have to do.
